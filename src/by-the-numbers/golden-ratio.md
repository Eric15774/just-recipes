## What Is the Golden Ratio?

The golden ratio (φ ≈ 1.618) is a mathematical constant that appears throughout nature and has been used in art and architecture for millennia. Applying it to cooking is more conceptual than scientific, but it can be an interesting guideline for balancing flavors and proportions.

**Important note:** Most traditional cooking ratios come from practical experience, not mathematical constants. The examples below are experiments, not established culinary standards. Take them with a proverbial grain of salt. Your mileage may vary.

## Bread and Baking

**Artisan bread hydration:** 1.618 parts flour to 1 part water. Bread ratios vary widely. Michael Ruhlman's 5:3 flour to water (about 1.67:1) is more commonly used.

**Cake flour balance:** 1.618 parts all-purpose flour to 1 part cake flour. A balance between structure and tenderness.

**Sourdough starter feeding:** 1.618 parts flour to 1 part starter, plus water to your usual hydration. Keeps the yeast active while developing flavor.

## Beverages

**Coffee:** 16.18 parts water to 1 part coffee, by weight. This falls within the standard 15:1 to 17:1 range for drip coffee.

**Tea blending:** 1.618 parts base tea to 1 part flavorings (herbs, spices, dried fruit). Lets the tea come through while adding complexity.

**Smoothies:** 1.618 parts fruit to 1 part liquid (milk, juice, yogurt). Thick and flavorful without being too thin.

**Lemonade:** 1.618 parts water to 1 part lemon juice plus simple syrup. More concentrated than most recipes, with a balanced sweet tart flavor.

**Iced tea concentrate:** 1.618 parts hot water to 1 part tea by weight, then dilute. A strong base that holds its flavor over ice.

**Chai spice mix:** 1.618 parts warming spices (cardamom, cinnamon) to 1 part bold spices (cloves, black pepper).

**Hot chocolate:** 1.618 parts milk to 1 part chocolate and cocoa mixture. Rich but not overwhelming.

## Seasoning and Spices

**Herb blends:** 1.618 parts primary herb to 1 part secondary herbs. The main flavor leads while the others support it.

**Spice rubs:** 1.618 parts sweet spices to 1 part heat. Warmth and complexity with controlled heat.

## Salads and Dressings

**Vinaigrette:** 1.618 parts oil to 1 part acid. Noticeably tangier than the traditional 3:1.

**Salad composition:** 1.618 parts greens to 1 part mix-ins (proteins, nuts, fruit). Keeps the greens primary while leaving room for variety.

## Proteins and Mains

**Marinades:** 1.618 parts oil to 1 part acid and aromatics. Tenderizes without making proteins mushy.

**Braising liquid:** 1.618 parts stock to 1 part wine. The wine enhances rather than dominates.

**Meatballs:** 1.618 parts meat to 1 part breadcrumb mixture. Tender while still holding together.

## Why This Might Work

The golden ratio shows up in nature as an efficient, pleasing proportion. In cooking it may create a balance that feels "just right," where neither ingredient overpowers the other but there is still enough contrast to be interesting.

## Disclaimers

1. **No scientific backing.** There is little evidence that the golden ratio makes food taste better.
2. **Traditional ratios work.** Established ratios exist for practical reasons and have been tested for generations.
3. **Taste is personal.** What works for one person may not work for another.
4. **Context matters.** Ingredient quality, cooking method, and cultural preference all change how a ratio works. This page is for experimenting and exploring.

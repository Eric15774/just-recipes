Making your own starter is not difficult, but if you know someone who has one, it is certainly easier to get some that way.

## Ingredients

- Fresh, wholesome flour (I prefer an organic whole wheat of some kind). A 5 pound bag is plenty, and 2 or 3 will do the trick.
- Water

## Directions

1. Make a doughball about 2" in diameter and bury it in the flour in a glass gallon jar. This stuff is really not fussy about the container. Leave it lightly covered on the counter at room temperature.
2. Every day, take out the doughball and cut or break off the crusty parts. Add a little water to make it squishy again, then knead in a little of the flour from the jar. Do this for 5 to 7 days at a minimum. The doughball will get a yeasty smell as the natural cultures build up strength.
3. Once you think you have a good culture going, mush the ball up completely with water, stir it up well in a jar, and spoon in some fresh flour.
4. You can feed it white flour or whatever once it is going. I feed mine daily on the counter, or weekly if I keep it in the refrigerator. Don't forget about it for too long.
5. You can stir in the liquid that forms on top, or pour it off and add some fresh water and new flour to feed the culture.

## Notes

- This is a very forgiving process. Just feed it once in a while and it will thrive.
